Abstract

The Rise of ICE and the Carceral State of Mahoning County

Mahoning County, Ohio: operating budget, checkbook, payroll, and revenue, FY2023–FY2026

Mahoning County's records describe two separate corrections operations. The county jail houses detainees, and the revenue booked to its criminal-justice fund rose sharply after ICE intake began in spring 2025. The county pays its own staff and vendors for that work. The Northeast Ohio Correctional Center is a private federal-contract prison whose beds the county pays for through the U.S. Marshals Service IGA fund, with federal receipts on the other side. This report measures each facility on its own records.

About one-fifth of county operating spending goes to what this report calls the carceral state — the jail, the federal-contract prison, and the public-safety functions around them — measured two overlapping ways. The federal relationship is not a one-year anomaly: ICE-prefixed payments began in April 2025, reached 26.3% of the fund's CoreCivic payments in partial FY2026, and the county's revised budgets carry $10 million of Housing Prisoner revenue into FY2027. The records cannot show intent. They show direction: the county is the fiscal channel for a growing federal detention arrangement, and the budget carries it forward.

Findings

What do the county's records establish about its two corrections operations?

The Mahoning County Jail cost $21,945,962.07 to operate in FY2025. Prisoner-housing revenue booked to the Criminal and Admin Justice Fund was $8,475,028.44, or 38.6% of that cost. The pre-ICE baseline for those receipts was $5,845,211.58 a year. FY2025 receipts ran $2,629,816.86 above it, and FY2026 is annualizing to $9,372,847.68, or $3,527,636.10 above the baseline. That increment is the cushion. Jail operating cost rose $2,072,772.39 over the same comparison, leaving $557,044.47 of the increment beyond that rise.

The same ICE-era work at NEOCC runs the other way. The county paid CoreCivic $42,587,206.23 from the US Marshals Service IGA fund in FY2025 and received $43,444,143.85 into it, leaving $856,937.62. In FY2026, payments exceed receipts by $2,145,659.99 through 2026-10-08. The fund carries a $0.00 revised appropriation and $43,211,748.72 in FY2025 actual spending.

The two flows are separate: the jail receives housing revenue against its own building and staff, while the IGA fund pays a contractor for beds at NEOCC. They appear in separate columns, against separate records.

Read together, they describe a direction. About one-fifth of county operating spending is corrections and public safety. At the jail, federal housing receipts rose after ICE intake began. At NEOCC, ICE-prefixed payments are now 26.3% of the fund's CoreCivic payments, and the county is the contracting party. The records cannot show why. They show the county making itself the fiscal channel for the arrangement, with revised budgets carrying the jail housing line into FY2027.

Two facilities, two directions

Which facility is costing the county, and which one is it paying for?

These are two different buildings, two different accounts, and two different directions of money.

The Mahoning County Jail is the county's own. The sheriff runs it, the county pays its staff, and the budget records its operating cost: $21,945,962.07 in FY2025. The jail receives prisoner-housing revenue—$8,475,028.44 in FY2025—booked to the Criminal and Admin Justice Fund. The revenue line names no payer, but the timing identifies the change: receipts averaged $487,100.97 a month before 2025, April 2025 brought $374,558.00, and May 2025 jumped to $914,014.88.

NEOCC is a private prison operated by CoreCivic under federal contracts. Its staff are not county employees and its costs are not county budget lines. The county pays CoreCivic through the US Marshals Service IGA fund: $42,587,206.23 in FY2025 and $156,074,778.24 over four years. Receipts to the same fund were $43,444,143.85 in FY2025, leaving $856,937.62. ICE-prefixed payments reached $10,423,700.24 in partial FY2026, or 26.3% of the fund's CoreCivic payments.

The IGA predates ICE. The records here establish timing and amounts, not a signing date or a document number.

At the jail, the money runs toward the county. At NEOCC, it runs through the county to a contractor. The branches below follow each facility.

What the jail costs

What does the jail cost, and what does a detainee-day cost?

The budget tree records $21,945,962.07 of jail operating cost in FY2025. Personal services are $14,395,276.30, contractual services $4,812,097.99, and materials and supplies $1,587,787.57; capital and other categories make up the rest.

Jail payroll is separate: 157 employees and $16,086,511.00 in FY2025, not a clean subset of the $14,395,276.30 personal-services line. The ledgers overlap without reconciling; adding them double-counts.

The jail costs that move with population are medical assistance and food: $4,400,340.59, or $24.31–$24.65 per day at average daily populations of 489 and 496. Adding overtime gives $5,421,828.27, or $29.95–$30.38 per day. What the jail returns

How much does the jail bring in, and what happened to the increase?

At the jail, prisoner-housing receipts were $5,873,466.71 in FY2023 and $5,816,956.45 in FY2024. The two-year average, $5,845,211.58, is the pre-ICE baseline. Receipts rose to $8,475,028.44 in FY2025, an increment of $2,629,816.86. ICE detainees arrived in late March 2025; revised contracts were ratified that September. After the agreement, monthly receipts averaged $862,505.20 — $10,350,062.39 annualized, an increment of $4,504,850.81. FY2026 receipts of $7,810,706.40 through October annualize to $9,372,847.68, $3,527,636.10 above baseline.

The fund's net position tells the same story: +$4,480,728.05 in FY2023, +$3,685,928.76 in FY2024, and +$3,669,137.87 in FY2025. Through 2026-10-16, FY2026 is at −$1,010,471.43.

The increase is accounted for. From FY2024 to FY2025, Housing Prisoner receipts rose $2,658,071.99 and permissive state sales tax rose $1,046,763.05; every other line in the fund nets to −$158,308.25. Federal grants did not fill the gap: Byrne JAG receipts were $59,138.50 in FY2025 and $17,520.78 in partial FY2026, and the eight criminal-justice grant funds fell from $1,997,151.96 in FY2023 to $649,288.42 in FY2025. Those are separate special-revenue funds, and no operating transfer entered the fund after FY2023.

The increase was spent in the same fund. From FY2024 to FY2025 the Sheriff's spending rose $3,006,175.53, and County Jail operating cost rose $2,072,772.39 on an all-funds basis. The $2,629,816.86 increment alone exceeds the jail's rise by $557,044.47; the Housing Prisoner and sales-tax increases together exceed the fund's entire spending increase. January–September payroll rose $1,540,070.07, checkbook spending $854,053.01, and capital outlay $668,069.13. The largest single item is a $279,831.00 BearCat bought on 2026-04-16. The jail organization shows $926,722.54 more, $4,991,281.50 to $5,918,004.04.

The receipts carry no payer field, and no expenditure line carries a funding source back to them. The money lands in the Criminal and Admin Justice Fund as a pool appropriated across criminal-justice agencies; no narrower restriction appears in the budget record, and federal conditions sit outside these ledgers. The records support a fund-level statement, not a line-level one: they cannot show that a particular check was paid from ICE receipts. What they show is direction: the only material revenue increases are Housing Prisoner receipts and sales tax, grants and transfers did not grow, and spending rose in the Sheriff and the jail. The best-supported reading is that added housing money and ordinary sales-tax growth funded the added spending — an inference from same-fund, same-window movements, not a line-by-line attribution.

NEOCC: the pass-through

Who pays for NEOCC, and what does the county keep?

The county does not run NEOCC. It pays CoreCivic through the US Marshals Service IGA fund, and federal receipts fill that fund.

In FY2025 the county paid CoreCivic $42,587,206.23 from the IGA fund and received $43,444,143.85 into it, leaving $856,937.62. The four-year contract totals $156,074,778.24. The margin did not grow with ICE. Against the FY2023–FY2024 average, FY2025 receipts rose $4,577,466.70 while USMS-attributed payments fell $2,011,009.01; the net position fell $1,047,718.10, from $1,904,655.72 at baseline to $856,937.62.

ICE-prefixed payments first appear on 2025-04-03. They were $7,636,193.81 in FY2025 and $10,423,700.24 in FY2026 through 2026-09-24, or 26.3% of the fund's CoreCivic payments. The USMS agreement is the channel through which NEOCC invoices are paid regardless of who is held there (Jameson 2021).

In FY2026, NEOCC payments of $39,563,360.85 exceed all federal receipts of $37,417,700.86 by $2,145,659.99 through 2026-10-08. The fund's revised appropriation is $0.00, against $43,211,748.72 of actual FY2025 spending. The shortfall is cash outrunning cash in, not an overrun against a published limit. A supplemental appropriation or transfer would legalize the spending; it would not create cash.

The receipts side is not labeled by agency, so the ledger cannot split ICE from USMS money in the fund.

The ledger shows alignment even where it cannot show intent. ICE-prefixed payments are more than a quarter of the fund's CoreCivic payments in partial FY2026, the county became the contracting party after the September 2025 ratification, and the fund is paying out faster than it takes in. That is what a deepening fiscal relationship looks like in a ledger. Whether it becomes permanent is not a ledger fact; it is a budget and policy question.

Corrections and public safety are about one-fifth of that spending on two overlapping measures. Public Safety is $87,416,426.53, or 20.06% of FY2025 actual spending by function. Criminal and Admin Justice ($42,668,377.10) plus the US Marshals Service IGA fund ($43,211,748.72) come to $85,880,125.82, or 19.70% by fund. They overlap — jail costs sit in both — and are not additive.

The largest reported function is UNDEFINED at $134,473,861.49, or 30.85% of operating spending, a label for spending filed by fund rather than function. It covers utilities ($57,534,263.76), a health pool ($30,217,195.84), roads ($20,637,473.93); only $42,605 is classified JAIL/MINIMUM SECURITY. The IGA fund is the clearest budget anomaly: a $0.00 revised appropriation against $43,211,748.72 of FY2025 actual spending.

Outside the operating budget, the Western Reserve Port Authority received 67 payments totaling $14,253,872.39 across the four years, rising to $6,910,783.24 in partial FY2026. Commissioner Capital Projects accounts for 62 of those lines, about $12.9 million; the largest purposes are the Government Center ($6,691,843.00) and Veterans ($5,636,798.36). These are county payments, not the authority's budget. The ledgers here do not show its own budget, its airport leases, or any state or JobsOhio money. Whether those dealings warrant scrutiny is a question for the authority's records, not a finding in these datasets.

That is the county's carceral state in fiscal terms: the jail, the federal-contract prison, and the public-safety and criminal-justice functions that hold, move, and process people. It takes about one dollar in five of operating money. Public Safety is the largest named function in FY2025 once the UNDEFINED block is set aside. The federal side is not a one-year entry: the jail's Housing Prisoner line is budgeted at $10,000,000 in revised FY2026, the all-funds FY2027 budget repeats that figure, and ICE-prefixed payments at NEOCC are a growing share of the fund's CoreCivic payments. That is the shape of entrenchment — not a single decision, but a federal role on both sides of the ledger, carried into the next budget. Whether it is intended as permanent is not a ledger fact.

Glossary

Load-bearing terms. Each definition is versioned and attached to this report; a change to a definition belongs in the errata.

Checkbook v1 · 2026-10-16
Mahoning County's transaction-level spending export: one row per check or electronic payment, with fund, department, function, object, vendor, and amount. It excludes payroll.
Payroll v1 · 2026-10-16
The county's payroll records: one row per pay line per employee, with department, job title, subcategory, and compensation components. It is a separate dataset from the checkbook; the two are not additive.
Operating budget v1 · 2026-10-16
The county's adopted and revised operating budget for funds, functions, departments, and objects, taken from the uncapped chart_data tree rather than the capped CSV export.
Revised budget v1 · 2026-10-16
The current amended budget figure published for a fiscal year. The source does not publish an original adopted figure alongside it.
Actual v1 · 2026-10-16
The amount the county reports as actually spent or collected in a period, as distinct from the revised budget.
Fund v1 · 2026-10-16
An accounting pot with its own constraints, such as the General Fund, the US Marshals IGA, or Criminal & Admin Justice. Money in one fund is not interchangeable with another.
Function v1 · 2026-10-16
The service classification of a budget or checkbook row, such as Public Safety or Health. In the checkbook it is derived from Segment2 because the published FunctionDescription field is blank.
UNDEFINED v2 · 2026-10-17
The published label for spending the county files by fund rather than by function. It is not missing data: it is the fund-based part of the budget, led by the sanitary sewer and water utilities, the self-insured health-care pool, and roads. The report defines the category in step 3 and keeps it visible rather than dropping or redistributing it.
Department v1 · 2026-10-16
The organizational unit that incurred payroll or budget in the source. Department names are as published and are not a clean hierarchy.
VendorId v1 · 2026-10-16
The county vendor-master identifier attached to a payee. One VendorId can carry several raw names, and a redacted line can share an ID with a named vendor; the report shows both the ID and the raw name.
Raw vendor name v1 · 2026-10-16
The vendor name string exactly as published in the checkbook, without normalization or alias merging. Spelling variants are separate strings.
REDACTED VENDOR v1 · 2026-10-16
The label the county publishes in place of a vendor name on some checkbook lines. The amount is public; the recipient is not.
IGA v1 · 2026-10-16
Intergovernmental agreement. In this report it is the US Marshals Service agreement under which the county houses federal detainees; its receipts appear in revenue as PUBLIC SAFETY / REVENUE NONOPERATING and its payments run through the IGA fund.
USMS v1 · 2026-10-16
United States Marshals Service, the federal agency whose detainees the county houses under the IGA.
ICE v1 · 2026-10-16
U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement. In the checkbook, ICE appears as a prefix on certain CoreCivic payment descriptions beginning 2025-04-03; the prefix is the county's own label, not a separate contract document.
CoreCivic v2 · 2026-10-17
The private prison operator paid by the county under VendorId 9056 (raw name CORECIVIC, INC.), the county's largest checkbook vendor over FY2023–FY2026. It operates the Northeast Ohio Correctional Center (NEOCC) in Youngstown; it does not operate the Mahoning County Jail.
NEOCC v1 · 2026-10-17
The Northeast Ohio Correctional Center, a private federal-contract prison in Youngstown operated by CoreCivic, Inc. The county pays CoreCivic through the US Marshals Service IGA fund; NEOCC is not the Mahoning County Jail, and CoreCivic staff are not county employees.
County jail (Mahoning County Jail) v1 · 2026-10-17
The sheriff-operated Mahoning County Jail. Its staff are county employees paid through payroll, and its ICE per-diem revenue is booked to the Criminal and Admin Justice Fund. It is a different facility and a different budget from NEOCC, which CoreCivic operates under contract.
Net amount v1 · 2026-10-16
Debits minus credits on a set of checkbook lines: payments minus refunds or reimbursements. A negative net means credits exceeded payments.
Jail-side payroll v2 · 2026-10-17
The three sheriff departments treated as jail operations in this report: SHERIFF - JAIL, SHERIFF - JAIL ADMINISTRATION, and SHERIFF - CRT SECURITY & ANNEX. These departments staff the Mahoning County Jail; none of this payroll is for NEOCC. It is a department assignment, not a line-item allocation.
IGA net position v2 · 2026-10-17
IGA receipts minus CoreCivic payments booked to the IGA fund in a fiscal year. Both sides describe the NEOCC contract. A difference between two datasets, not profit: jail payroll and other county costs are not subtracted, and it says nothing about the Mahoning County Jail.
Housing Prisoner v2 · 2026-10-17
Revenue account 22230 in the Criminal and Admin Justice Fund, character code 12 intergovernmental, booked to the Justice Fund Revenue organization. It carries payments for housing prisoners in the county jail. It is revenue to the sheriff-run Mahoning County Jail, not a payment for NEOCC. External reporting places ICE detainees there from April 2025 at $125 per inmate per day, but the line has no payer field and predates that agreement, so it also carries other prisoner-housing revenue.
FY2026 partial v1 · 2026-10-16
Fiscal year 2026 as covered by the data: checkbook and payroll through 2026-10-16. FY2026 totals are not comparable to full years.

What this report cannot see

Named gaps, located and treated without interpolation. None of these is filled with an estimate unless the estimate is itself labeled.

  1. Some spending has a fund but no function

    Where: Operating budget by function; checkbook by function

    30.85% of FY2025 operating budget actual and 34.66% of FY2025 checkbook net carry the function label UNDEFINED. These are funds that do their own work and are not assigned to one of the county's named functions, so the function view cannot say what service the money bought.

    Treatment: Rendered as its own category and defined in step 7: seven fund groups, led by sewer and water utilities, health-care pooling, and roads. It is never dropped or redistributed. The checkbook's UNDEFINED share is computed on a different measure and is close but not identical.

    Affects: FY2025 actual spending under UNDEFINED, UNDEFINED share of FY2025 actual operating spending, FY2025 UNDEFINED spending by fund group, What sits under UNDEFINED, FY2025, UNDEFINED share of FY2025 checkbook net.

  2. Redacted vendors

    Where: Checkbook vendor

    33,716 checkbook lines totaling $47,125,237.12 are labeled REDACTED VENDOR across FY2023–FY2026.

    Treatment: Amounts are counted; recipients are not attributed. Some redacted lines share a VendorId with a named vendor, so a VendorId join can attach a redacted payment to a named entity; the index shows both fields.

    Affects: Checkbook lines labeled REDACTED VENDOR, Net spending labeled REDACTED VENDOR.

  3. FY2026 is incomplete

    Where: Every time series

    Checkbook and payroll rows end 2026-10-16; the budget and revenue records cover the year only to the same date.

    Treatment: FY2026 is marked partial in the running head and at every use; it is excluded from full-year comparisons.

    Affects: FY2026 actual operating spending, partial, Checkbook net spending by fiscal year, County payroll, total compensation by year, US Marshals IGA receipts by year.

  4. Unassigned IGA gap

    Where: US Marshals IGA fund, FY2025

    IGA budget actual is $624,542.49 higher than IGA checkbook payments in FY2025.

    Treatment: Shown as a difference with its cause unassigned; no accrual, timing, or classification explanation is asserted.

    Affects: IGA fund budget actual less fund checkbook, FY2025, US Marshals Service IGA fund, FY2025 actual, FY2025 checkbook net, US Marshals Service IGA fund.

  5. Housing Prisoner receipts have no payer

    Where: Criminal and Admin Justice Fund revenue, account 22230

    The HOUSING PRISONER account is a single intergovernmental revenue line with no payer field. External reporting places county-jail ICE detainees in the Criminal and Admin Justice Fund at $125 per inmate per day beginning April 2025, and monthly receipts are consistent with that rate, but the county data cannot separate ICE from other prisoner-housing payers, nor identify the pre-2025 payers.

    Treatment: The line is reported as a whole, and no payer split is estimated. The report does quantify the post-April-2025 step-up by comparing receipts against the FY2023–FY2024 average, which predates the agreement; that difference is attributed to the ICE-era intake by timing and size, and is labeled as timing evidence rather than a payer split. It assumes no other prisoner-housing payer changed at the same time, which the county record cannot rule out. The report cites the external $125-per-day agreement as context. The separate scale estimate multiplies a deliberately low hypothetical headcount by the reported per-diem — it is arithmetic, not a share of this line.

    Affects: Housing Prisoner revenue at the jail, by year, Housing Prisoner revenue by month, Housing Prisoner revenue at the jail, monthly average, FY2023–FY2024, Housing Prisoner annual average at the jail, FY2023–FY2024, Housing Prisoner revenue at the jail, monthly average after the April 2025 agreement, Housing Prisoner receipts, May–December 2025 annualized, Housing Prisoner revenue at the jail above the pre-agreement baseline, FY2025, Housing Prisoner revenue at the jail above the pre-agreement baseline, May–December 2025 annualized, Housing Prisoner revenue at the jail above the pre-agreement baseline, FY2026 annualized.

  6. The port authority's own records are absent

    Where: Western Reserve Port Authority payments; every county ledger

    The county checkbook records 67 payments to VendorId 12693 totaling $14,253,872.39. No receipts from the authority, transfer back to the county, authority payroll, or contract record appears in the county datasets. The checkbook shows the county's outbound payments, not the authority's budget, spending, projects, or revenues, and no line connects the authority to the airport or to immigration detention.

    Treatment: The report states this boundary and names the records that would close it — the authority's budget, audited financial statements, board minutes, and agreements. No inference about airport or ICE activity is drawn from county payment data.

    Affects: Four-year payments to the Western Reserve Port Authority, Western Reserve Port Authority payments by purpose, Western Reserve Port Authority payments by year.
